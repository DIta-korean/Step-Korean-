One system, three moving parts

Every Hangul syllable is built the same way: an initial consonant, a vowel, and — often — a final consonant, stacked into one square block. Learn the shapes once and you can read any block in the language, the same way you'd sound out a new word in English once you know the alphabet. That's the whole lesson below, in order: vowels, consonants, then how they combine.

Step 6

Say who you are

One pattern gets you a full sentence: 저는 [이름]이에요 / 예요 — "I am [name]." Which ending you use depends on the very last sound of the name.

Name ends in a consonant sound 저는 민준이에요 I am Minjun.
Name ends in a vowel sound 저는 수아예요 I am Sua.

Try it: swap in your own name using the sound it ends on, not the spelling.
